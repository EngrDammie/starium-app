Application · Chief Software Architect

Your next chapter deserves more than software. It deserves a proven builder.

A letter to the leadership of Eko Organic Food Industries Limited (EKOFIN) — written by the engineer who built a complete factory digital platform from inside its floor, finished and proven, awaiting only the word to deploy.

Dear Sir/Madam,

Every great food company runs two businesses. The one that grows, processes and ships its products — and the quieter one that gathers what all of it means. The first business you have built impressively: two divisions, six sourcing states, a modern plant in Epe, kernels moving toward the world. The second business — the one made of data — is where most companies quietly bleed, and it is precisely there that I have spent my career preparing to serve you.

My name is Damilare Paul Ayodele, and I am writing to offer myself to EKOFIN as your Chief Software Architect — not as a man with theories, but as a builder with receipts. I am currently a Quality Control officer at Starium Rafa Detergents, where I have served since February 2026. I am also the person who, outside my official duties, single-handedly designed, built and deployed a complete enterprise resource planning platform for that factory — fully functional, running live in my own test environments today, and ready to serve its operations the moment management approves deployment.

01 The Problem I Could Not Ignore

I did not find the problem in a boardroom. I found it standing at a machine, holding a clipboard. Every shift, critical numbers — densities, weights, waste, machine stoppages, pallet movements — were captured by hand into paper notebooks, transcribed hours later, aggregated days later, and acted upon… sometimes never. Managers were making decisions from memory. Supervisors walked the floor merely to discover what was already true an hour ago. And whenever the internet failed — as it faithfully does in any factory — the little digitisation that existed simply stopped.

I realised the gap between what was happening on the floor and what leadership knew was not a people problem. It was a software problem. And nobody was coming to solve it. So, on my own time, at my own expense, I began to solve it myself.

02 What I Built — While Doing My Day Job

The Starium Rafa Enterprise ERP is complete and running live in my test environments today: twenty-plus routes covering the operational spine of a manufacturing company, real-time on every device, resilient against power and network failure, and costing nothing in licences or servers. It is finished, proven end-to-end, and ready to enter daily factory service as soon as management approves its deployment.

Powder Density Command

Level 9 & BOT lines — live density validation, machine grids, executive charts, digital approvals.

QC Sachet Suite

String weight, bag & carton inspections with cooldown logic, batch lineage and dual sign-off.

Waste Intelligence

Carton & laminate waste tracked per machine, per round, with thresholds, alerts and trend analytics.

Silos & Machine Health

Empty-silo detection, stopped-machine issues, downtime history, automated broadcast alerts.

Pallet Transfer Ledger

Floor-to-warehouse movement with auto-computed cartons, teams and waybill-ready quantities.

Governance Layer

Three-tier role-based access, live presence, audit-friendly records, boardroom-grade reports with CSV/print.

Underneath it sits an offline-first architecture: every entry is preserved locally the instant it is made, then synchronises automatically when connectivity returns. Zero data loss has gone from an aspiration to a property of the system. It is hosted redundantly across three independent providers — Firebase, GitHub Pages and Cloudflare Workers Pages — because in manufacturing, availability is not a feature; it is the product.

03 Imagine

Imagine your weighing bridges, grading lines, blending halls and packaging bays all speaking one digital language — every kernel weighed, every batch graded, every carton sealed streaming to your screen the very second it happens, from the farm-gates of six states to the export dock at Epe.

Imagine leadership unchained from distance. From Lagos, London or Dubai, you watch the facility breathe in real time — procurement volumes climbing, quality checks passing, containers closing toward Rotterdam — while stale figures, lost booklets and Excel graveyards become relics your competitors still depend on.

Imagine your buyers — commodity houses abroad, retailers at home — placing orders, tracing shipments back to the very farming communities that grew them, and verifying certificates through world-class portals that carry the EKOFIN name with pride.

None of this requires waiting for the future. Every piece of it is technology I have already shipped once — built inside a real factory's walls, under real constraints, proven end-to-end in my own live test environments, on my own initiative. It is what I intend to bring to your table.

04 Why EKOFIN. Why Now.

EKOFIN is standing at the exact moment where software stops being a convenience and becomes strategy. The lease of the Julius Berger 60-tonne plant multiplies your processing capacity; multiplied capacity without multiplied traceability is risk, not growth. Your public commitments — rigorous testing, traceability checks, certified quality, NAFDAC compliance, international food-safety standards — are, at their core, promises that only systems can keep at scale. Export buyers will increasingly ask not just what you shipped, but prove it: which farm, which batch, which test, which date. The companies that answer instantly will win the contracts.

And there is a simpler truth: I live in Epe. Your facility is minutes from my home. The success of this endeavour would not be a contract to me — it would be the transformation of my own community's greatest industrial asset, led from within.
