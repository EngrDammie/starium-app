The Man Who Built Starium's Digital System Needs a Proper Tool

A short, honest explanation of the one laptop request behind the Starium Rafa ERP — a finished system waiting for management's approval, and the person who will deploy and maintain it.

Imagine every manager and supervisor in this factory opening their phone or computer to see — live — the quality tests, waste figures, stopped machines and pallet movements of the moment, even when the internet fails. That system exists today: the Starium Rafa Enterprise ERP, designed and built by one of our own QC staff on his personal laptop, outside working hours. It is finished and running in his test environment — ready to be deployed across our operations as soon as management approves.

This page explains, without technical jargon, why upgrading that laptop is one of the smallest investments this factory can make with one of its largest returns.

The Short Version
One strong business laptop costs about ₦2 – ₦3 million, lasts four to five years, works out to roughly ₦50,000 – ₦60,000 per month — and powers the system that is ready to save our managers hours every single week.
Why It Matters Here

The ERP is ready to enter daily operations

QC records, machine statuses, waste tracking and reports will all flow through it from day one. Deploying, fixing and improving it starts at his keyboard.

Staff training is coming

Every department will be brought onto the system. Training sessions need the computer running smoothly in front of an audience.

Demos win confidence

Videos and live presentations of dashboards are how management sees value. A freezing laptop mid-presentation says the wrong thing.

He walks our floor

The laptop travels into dusty halls and hot lines during shift handovers — it must survive bumps, heat and long days away from plugs.

Power cuts are normal here

A good battery plus phone-style charging means work continues through outages instead of stopping.

Our data must stay ours

Fingerprint locks and encryption keep factory information safe even if the device is lost.

Fingerprint & encryptionFactory secrets stay locked even if the laptop disappears. Only the owner's finger opens it.
WarrantyIf anything breaks, the maker fixes it free — bought properly from official Nigerian dealers.

Fair Questions, Honest Answers
"Hasn't he managed fine so far?"
He has — building the whole system alone on a personal machine. But building was phase one; deploying and maintaining it for the whole company is phase two, and that deserves a dependable, warrantied tool rather than borrowed equipment.
"What happens if the old laptop fails during rollout?"
Days or weeks of delay to deployment, training and fixes — with no warranty safety net. One failed part could pause progress across several departments at once.
"Does the company own what he builds?"
The intent has always been for the system to belong to Starium Rafa. Equipping the builder properly formalises that arrangement and protects company data on company hardware.
"Any monthly cost?"
None. No subscriptions, no licences, no fees. One purchase, four to five years of service.
"Where would it be bought?"
From official Lagos channels — TD Africa or iStore — with proper invoices and warranty papers. Prices checked August 2026.
