Application for the Position of Fullstack Web Developer — Building the Quiet Infrastructure the Next Economy Depends On

Dear Hiring Team,

Your careers page says you would rather a candidate introduce himself directly — and that your culture is built on clarity of writing, autonomy, and trust. So here is that introduction, written as plainly as I know how. I am a full-stack web developer with more than ten years of shipped software behind me, and I am applying for the Fullstack Web Developer role described on your job board because — reading how you build — I recognised your method as the one I have been practising alone, for the last year, in a factory on the other side of the world.

While working a full-time Quality Control shift at Starium Rafa Detergents in Epe, Lagos, I watched critical production data vanish into paper notebooks night after night while managers decided from memory. So I built the fix myself — outside working hours, at zero software cost — and shipped a complete enterprise platform: a 20+ module, offline-first web application covering quality control, batch lineage, waste and yield intelligence, machine and silo health, and pallet and stores ledger, with role-based access control and boardroom-grade dashboards. It is live today at starium-app.dammieoptimus.workers.dev, running on triple-redundant hosting, built as a single-page React application with a real-time data engine that keeps working when the internet drops. That is my working definition of uptime, performance and reliability — the very KPIs in your job description — proven under genuinely hostile network conditions.

Your portfolio makes the same point from a different direction. Veycl needs resilient journeys across borders and devices; Apply2unis needs multi-step flows, eligibility logic and forms that work perfectly on a phone in a village; Sellable Marketing needs pages that load fast, rank and convert. All three are, at heart, the same engineering I do every day: a responsive, performance-conscious front-end on top of a clean service layer and honest data modelling. I handle the full stack — HTML, CSS, JavaScript, React on the front end; REST/JSON APIs on the back end (today in ASP.NET Core — portable to Node.js/Express in days, not months); SQL Server and MySQL for data; Git and CI/CD pipelines for delivery; semantic, metadata-minded markup and Core Web Vitals discipline for speed and SEO.

Imagine a single React codebase serving all three brands — Veycl's eligibility flows, Apply2unis' admissions journeys, Sellable Marketing's landing pages — with shared components, a shared design system and one pipeline shipping them all.

Imagine a candidate who has already run a live, multi-tenant web product alone — handling its uptime, its backups, its performance budgets and its user support — walking into your remote-first culture and simply being trusted with something that matters.

Imagine me in your first weekly standup: clear written updates, autonomous delivery, and a build that gets measurably faster, more reliable and better under the hood from the first sprint.

Here is how I would treat the first ninety days against the success criteria in your posting — uptime, features shipped, turnaround time, code quality, SEO:

  • First 30 days — learn your three brands from the inside: read every codebase, deploy the stack locally, ship a productive first task (a bug fix, a test, a performance improvement) within the first two weeks, and document what I learn in writing — clarity of writing, as your culture demands.
  • First 60 days — own a feature end-to-end: build, test, ship and monitor it, with clean commits and documented decisions; extend coverage with Jest/Mocha-style measures and harden CI/CD as needed.
  • First 90 days and beyond — deliver measurable improvements in a KPI I can defend with numbers (page performance, uptime, SEO score, turnaround time for a ticket class), then take increasing ownership on the path toward Senior then Lead Web Developer.

You will notice I have not claimed Node.js or MongoDB on a résumé of projects that don't use them — intellectual honesty is one of your stated values, and it costs me nothing to practise. What I can prove are a decade of REST APIs, relational data modelling, JavaScript and React at production scale, and the speed and hunger to reach full fluency in any stack your platform actually runs. I work autonomously, I communicate in writing without friction, and I already run a live web product as a fully remote operator — so your remote-first, async-friendly way of working is my default, not an adjustment.

My enclosed CV tells the fuller story; my certificates and the live system are one click away below; and my application is ready for the six-question screening step in your process, whenever you are. I would be glad to demo the live platform in a call — synchronous or recorded — at your convenience.

Yours faithfully,
