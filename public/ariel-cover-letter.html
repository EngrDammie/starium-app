Application for the Position of Chief Software Architect — Building the Data Spine That Helps Africa Feed Africa

Dear Sir/Madam,

I am writing to you from your own neighbourhood — I live in Iraye-Oke, Epe, minutes from your gates at Alaro City, where you operate the largest ready-to-use therapeutic food factory in Africa — to offer myself as your Chief Software Architect. What I bring is not a résumé of theories but a rare, concrete proof: while serving as a Quality Control officer at Starium Rafa Detergents, a factory floor I know the way you know yours, I personally designed, built and deployed a complete enterprise ERP for that company — fully functional, offline-resilient, running live in my own test environments today, and awaiting only management approval to enter daily operations, all at zero software cost to my employer.

That platform was born of the same conditions your plant guards against every day. I did not learn manufacturing's data problems from slides; I learned them holding a clipboard beside running machines. Every shift I watched critical figures — densities, weights, waste, machine stoppages, pallet movements — vanish into paper notebooks, transcribed late, aggregated later, acted upon rarely. In a detergent plant that gap costs money. For a producer of therapeutic food, where UNICEF, WFP and USAID require proof of every lot's moisture, aflatoxin and micronutrient results, that gap can cost a batch — and a batch is a child's recovery. I concluded that the distance between a factory floor and what leadership knows is fundamentally a software problem — and I solved it. The platform covers QC testing, inspection and batch lineage, waste and yield intelligence, machine and silo health, pallet and stores tracking, governance with three-tier role-based access, and boardroom-grade reporting — all synchronised in real time and hardened against power and connectivity failure through an offline-first architecture.

Why does this matter to Ariel Foods, beyond good engineering? Because you are standing at the precise moment where software stops being a convenience and becomes strategy. The US$2.5 million UNCDF investment under the UNCDF–UNICEF Nutrition Supplier Finance Facility is putting new equipment on your floor to process more locally grown peanuts, soy and chickpeas — bringing as many as 1,500 smallholder farmers into your chain and helping partners reach an estimated 25,000 more children every year by 2030. Imported peanuts arrive with certificates; local peanuts arrive with stories. Turning those stories into audit-grade, traceability-ready supply-chain data is the engineering I practise. Your accession to the UN Global Compact in May 2026 adds a public commitment to report progress against the Sustainable Development Goals — a system I can make effortless.

Imagine every peanut lot that passes through Alaro City—from a Kano smallholder's farm gate to the exact foil-sachet lot number — logged, weighed, screened, and traceable in seconds, for every audit and every tender.

Imagine roasters, blenders and fillers reporting their own health by the second, alerting an operator before a deviation becomes a disposal — and your team in Lagos, Nairobi or London watching production, yield and release rates live.

Imagine quality results captured on tablets at the lab bench, batch release approved with named digital signatures, and compliance packs — HACCP-ready, UNICEF-ready, NAFDAC-ready — generated in minutes, not days.

This future requires no waiting. Every component of it is technology I have already shipped once, built inside a real factory's walls under genuine constraints, and proven end-to-end in live testing. Given the mandate, here is how I would begin:

  • First 30 days — walk the full Alaro City chain (peanut intake, roasting and milling, blending, QC labs, packing, warehousing, dispatch to agencies and ports) and publish a current-state map with its cost in hours and errors per batch.
  • First 60 days — deliver visible quick wins: digital QC/lab and batch-lot records with offline capture, plus sachet and pallet tracking, adapted from a platform already proven end-to-end in live factory testing.
  • First 100 days — present the blueprint for Ariel's integrated core (inventory, batch genealogy, procurement, production orders, finance integration) plus executive dashboards for production versus plan, yield, QC pass rates and traceability coverage.

With over a decade of enterprise engineering — senior .NET development at The GIG Group's logistics systems, banking integrations at Longbridge, HR systems at Alliedsoft Xceed365, field hardware at EasyFuel — combined with my current life on a production floor and my leadership of a 700-member nutrition-focused network, I offer Ariel a builder who speaks both languages fluently: the language of machines, shifts and peanut yields, and the language of databases, dashboards and audit-ready records. My enclosed CV tells the fuller story; my references will confirm the character behind it.

I would be honoured to demonstrate the live system — floor, code and dashboards — at Alaro City at your convenience, and to discuss how this technology accelerates the mission your founder has stated so plainly: Africa serving Africa. Thank you for your time and consideration.

Yours faithfully,

Engr. Damilare Paul Ayodele
