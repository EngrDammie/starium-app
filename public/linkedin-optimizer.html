Headline Rules

  • Never use your current job title alone — it's a LinkedIn default, not a strategy
  • Front-load the most searchable term — "Software Architect" must appear in the first 60 characters (that's what shows in search results before the "...")
  • Use separators — the pipe | character creates visual breaks on mobile; it's the cleanest separator on LinkedIn
  • Include a call-to-action implicitly — words like "Available for" or "Open to" signal you're approachable
  • Never mention a company name that no longer employs you — it reads as outdated
📸Section 2 — Profile Photo & Banner Image

People decide whether to read your profile in 0.05 seconds. The photo and banner are what they see in that window.

Profile Photo — The Non-Negotiables

  1. Professional headshot only. No selfies, no group photos, no sunglasses, no cropped wedding pictures. A plain background (white, grey, or blurred office) with natural light.
  2. Face fills 60–70% of the frame. People must recognize you from 3 feet away — that's how it appears in search results and comment threads.
  3. Dress one level above your target. For ERP/consulting roles: a clean collared shirt or blazer. No tie needed — but look like someone a boardroom would trust.
  4. Smile genuinely. Not a grin — a slight, confident smile. Research shows profiles with warm expressions get 20% more connection requests.
  5. Update it now if your current photo is more than 2 years old. LinkedIn's algorithm literally boosts recently-updated profiles.

Banner Image — Your Free Billboard (1584 × 396 px)

Why Every Paragraph Works

  1. First line is a hook, not a bio. "I build the software that runs factories" is 8 words that create instant curiosity. It's not a job title — it's a declaration.
  2. The story creates empathy. "Paper notebooks... QC data vanished... decisions from memory" — anyone who's worked in a factory FEELS this. You're not listing skills — you're describing a problem you solved.
  3. The proof is specific and quantified. "zero licence cost... live test environments... offline-first" — concrete facts beat adjectives every time.
  4. The career narrative shows range. Banking → transportation → HR → manufacturing — not job-hopping, but breadth of domain understanding.
  5. The "what I do isn't just code" paragraph is your differentiator. This is where you separate yourself from the 10 million other developers on LinkedIn. You understand the BUSINESS, not just the technology.
  6. Skills are listed for search. LinkedIn's algorithm scans the About section for keywords. These bullet points ensure you appear in searches for ERP, manufacturing, traceability, quality management, etc.
  7. The call to action is clear. "Let's talk" with contact details — makes it easy for the right people to reach you.
🧠 Character count: The above is ~1,850 characters — well within LinkedIn's 2,600 limit. You have room to add a specific achievement or two if you want, but brevity wins on LinkedIn. Most people never scroll to the end.
