# Ariel Foods FZE – Company Profile  
Ariel Foods FZE is a **Kenyan-owned food manufacturer** now operating in Nigeria.  It specializes in **ready-to-use therapeutic foods (RUTF)** – high-calorie, vitamin‑fortified peanut-paste supplements for treating severe malnutrition.  In 2024–2025 Ariel Foods built a **state-of-the-art processing plant** in Alaro City (Lekki Free Zone, Lagos) and began production to serve West Africa.  The company is led by owner **Dhiren Chandaria** (who also owns Kenya’s Insta Products, a major UNICEF/WFP supplier).  Ariel Foods is registered in Nigeria (classified as an SME) and recently joined the UN Global Compact (May 2026) with a mission aligned to UN Sustainable Development Goals (nutrition, health, and agriculture).  

## Products, Markets & Impact  
Ariel Foods’ core products are **therapeutic food supplements** for malnourished children and at-risk adults.  These RUTF products are essentially **peanut paste fortified with sugar, oil, milk powder and 23 vitamins/minerals**.  They follow strict food-safety and hygiene standards (as noted by Insta Products, Ariel’s sister company, whose RUTF is used by UNICEF and World Food Programme).  The company’s **goals** include scaling production to meet humanitarian demand (for UNICEF, WFP, NGOs) and to improve “food and population security” in Africa.  

In Nigeria, Ariel Foods aims to **build local supply chains**.  A US$2.5 million UNCDF loan (2025) funded new processing equipment at the Lekki plant so more locally grown peanuts and legumes can be used.  This expansion is expected to create markets for **1,500+ Nigerian smallholder farmers** in northern regions and enable treatment of **~25,000 malnourished children annually by 2030**.  Nigerian authorities have publicly welcomed Ariel Foods’ investment as a vote of confidence in Alaro City and Lagos Free Zone.  In summary, Ariel Foods is a specialized agro‐food manufacturer focused on **nutritional aid products**, with global distribution and a growing footprint in West Africa.  

## Business Challenges & Digital Needs  
**Supply Chain Complexity:**  Sourcing high-quality peanuts and micronutrients across borders requires **traceability and compliance**.  Ariel Foods must ensure ingredients meet UNICEF/WFP standards and follow precise formulations.  (The UNCDF report notes they needed “specialized equipment” to process local peanuts.) A Software Architect could design a **traceability system** (e.g. blockchain or database) to track each batch from farm to factory, ensuring transparency and quality control.  

**Manufacturing Automation:**  The new factory is highly automated, but complex machinery (roasters, mixers, fillers) needs monitoring. A solutions architect could recommend an **IoT/SCADA system** to collect real-time data (temperatures, mixing ratios, machine status) and alert operators to anomalies, reducing downtime and scrap.  Predictive maintenance (using machine-learning on sensor data) could further cut unexpected breakdowns.  

**Inventory & ERP:**  Managing raw ingredients (peanuts, vitamins) and finished RUTF inventory is critical. Implementing an **ERP/MRP system** (e.g. SAP Business One, Odoo, Microsoft Dynamics) would integrate procurement, production planning, and finance.  This ensures optimal stock levels and automatic reordering (avoiding shortages of key nutrients) while providing financial controls.  

**Quality Assurance & Compliance:**  RUTF producers must follow strict food-safety regulations (HACCP, WHO guidelines). A custom **quality-management application** could document each lot’s test results, generate compliance reports for auditors, and help certify products for export.  

**Data & Analytics:**  To scale efficiently, Ariel Foods needs data-driven decision-making. For example, an AI-powered analytics dashboard could forecast demand (based on malnutrition trends or NGO orders) and optimize production schedules.  Machine learning could analyze production data to improve yields (e.g. fine-tuning roasting time based on peanut moisture).  

**Scalability & Cloud:**  As a fast-growing manufacturer with global partners, Ariel Foods would benefit from cloud infrastructure (AWS/Azure) for its IT systems.  Cloud-hosted applications allow remote monitoring of multiple sites, secure data backups, and easy scaling.  A chief software architect would design a scalable, secure architecture (with proper data encryption and user management) so the company can add new locations (e.g. Ghana, DR Congo, etc.) without overhauling IT.
